Smackfor Alchemy Lab

Catch what you just played. Cut it up. Let a seed decide the rest.

Racked and heard. Not everything tried yet.

On 22 September 2026 it ran in a rack on a powered Alchemy Lab and made sound: audio in, a loop captured, audio out. On the same module the SD picker switched between Smack, Mark and Belt and back, and Smack's settings were still there when it came back. On the bench, before racking, its own load meter read 3.3% peak at idle pass-through.

Not yet tried on the module: the pattern stepping with a clock on J3, fast rates included; the CV inputs J4 to J8; and a cold power cycle keeping its settings. What this page says about those is what the code does and what the native tests show, not what has been heard.

What it does

01 — The idea

Audio runs through the module continuously and is always being recorded. Hold CAPTURE and the last LENGTH steps of what you just played become a loop, cut into slices, with a seeded pattern of effects running across them. No arming, no deciding in advance that you wanted it.

Retro-capture

You never miss it

The good take is usually the one you weren't recording. The buffer is always rolling, 150 seconds of it, so CAPTURE reaches backwards instead of forwards.

Seeded

One number, whole arrangement

Which slices get re-ordered, which get an effect, and which effect all follow from a single SEED. Same seed, same result, every time. Tap CAPTURE to roll another.

Density, not editing

Steer the statistics

Six pots cannot edit slices one at a time, so they steer the probabilities instead: how much re-ordering, how many effects, how fine the slicing.

It is the same C engine as Smack for the Ableton Move and Smack Versio, recompiled for the Alchemy Lab: six pots on two pages, three buttons, six CV jacks that read independently of the pots, and 102 LEDs to say what is going on. It also brings something the engine never had a place for before: firmware swapping from the SD card, so this module can carry several firmwares and change between them in a settings page, each keeping its own settings.

Playing it

03 — Five steps to the first loop
1  Patch audio to J1 / J2 (IN). It passes through immediately —
   the module is not silent before you touch it.
2  Patch a clock to J3. No clock? It free-runs. That is fine.
3  Play something.
4  Hold B1 CAPTURE about a second. The last LENGTH steps become
   a loop and start playing.
5  Bring up FX and ORDER. Hold B3 and turn P1 for a new SEED.

Tap CAPTURE to re-roll: same loop, new pattern. Re-roll is on the tap because it is the gesture you use constantly while playing; grabbing a loop is deliberate and happens once, so it gets the hold. Hold past two seconds to drop the loop and return to dry pass-through.

LIVE mode — double-tap CAPTURE

The B1 light turns cyan. Instead of repeating one frozen take, the module re-captures itself once per loop pass, so the buffer keeps refilling with what you are playing now.

LENGTH sets how often that happens, and the wait is a full loop pass. At 120 BPM that is about a second at 8 steps and half a minute at 256, where it looks completely dead for thirty seconds. If you want LIVE to feel live, turn LENGTH down.

It records your dry input, never the effected output, so passes never compound and the loop cannot degrade into mush.

BLEND is a real dry/wet. It crossfades the loop against the live input in the audio callback, and it is bypassed until a loop exists, so the module is never silent before you have captured anything.

Very slow clocks shorten the longest LENGTH. A loop may take at most half the 150-second buffer, because the recorder has to keep writing a fresh loop alongside the one that is playing. Below about 51 BPM, which CLOCK RATIO at ×2 reaches from a 100 BPM clock, LENGTH 256 drops a notch rather than being trimmed off-grid. It stays dropped until the pot moves.

LENGTH, SLICE and SEED are stepped, and a stepped control parked exactly on a boundary would otherwise chatter between two values as noise, or CV, nudges it back and forth. Each has a deadband. SEED's is exactly one step wide, because a single step of SEED re-rolls the whole pattern and a wider band would make the pot skip most of the 128 seeds.

The second page, the punch, the filter

04 — What B3, B2 and the bottom-right pot buy back

The Versio had seven knobs, two switches and one button and still ran out of panel. Here the pots have a second page and the buttons have one job each, so everything the Versio hid behind a triple-tap is simply on the panel.

Opening the page to look cannot change anything. The SDK's pot catch runs on the way in and on the way out: a SETUP pot only takes effect once you turn it through the value it already holds, and letting go of B3 hands the pots back to the PLAY page without jumping anything. Both pages are saved to flash a few seconds after you stop moving a pot, and come back the next time Smack starts.

The PLAY page comes back where you left it, and the pots catch it. The six pots are shared by every firmware on the card, so after switching back from Mark or Belt they point wherever that firmware left them. Smack does not adopt those positions: each PLAY pot keeps its saved value and takes over only once you turn it through that value, the same catch the SETUP page uses. The one exception is the first boot after flashing, when nothing is saved yet and the PLAY page takes whatever the pots point at.

First boot after flashing seeds the SETUP page with SEED 0, a 12-semitone pitch range, ratio =1, punch RETRIG, stereo, 120 BPM.

PUNCH — hold B2

Hold it and every slice is forced through the effect chosen on SETUP's P4; release and the pattern snaps back. The B1 light goes white while it is held. Nothing else is on B2, so it can be played hard: a punch you have to think about is not a punch.

Punch CLEAN is the one to try first. It is the bottom of the P4 list, and instead of adding an effect it momentarily drops the glitch pattern, so a dense loop snaps back to unmangled for as long as you hold. Above it, in order: Retrig, Reverse, Pitch, Speed, Gate, Buzz, Crush, Repeat, Rev After, Tape Stop, Tape Start, Scratch, Env, Pan, Filter, Vowel, Tonal Delay, Freeze, Delay, Dist, Phaser, Verb, P-Shift, Ring Mod, Comb, Scatter.

One consequence of B2 also being half of the Settings chord: holding B2+B3 punches for the two seconds before Settings opens, then lets go. That is the price of keeping punch available on the SETUP page.

The DJ filter — P6 on the PLAY page

Left sweeps a lowpass down, right sweeps a highpass up, and the centre is a real bypass: a notch wide enough to find by feel on a pot with no detent. The ring shows it as blue to the left, orange to the right, and a single white LED at the top when you are in the notch.

It runs last, after BLEND, across the module's whole output, so it works on your dry input before you have captured anything at all, which nothing else on the panel does. Measured natively on the same filter code: −56 dB at 1 kHz fully left, −72 dB at 100 Hz fully right. At exactly zero the block is skipped altogether rather than run "open", because even open the filter shaved half a decibel off 8 kHz.

On the Versio this filter had to share a knob with PITCH RANGE. Here each has its own.

How a pattern is rolled

05 — FX density, ORDER density and the seed, run for real

A pattern is two passes over the slices with one seeded random stream. ORDER goes first: each slice, in turn, has an ORDER-density chance of trading places with a random slice. Then FX: each slice has an FX-density chance of getting an effect, and if it does, which one is a uniform pick from all 26, followed by a roll of that effect's own setting. The densities decide how many; the seed decides which.

The grid below runs the engine's roll, ported line for line with its generator, so for a given SEED with no re-rolls it is the pattern the module will play. Cells with a colour have an effect; a dashed cell plays a different slice from the one that would be there in the original order, and the big number is the slice you hear.

Why ORDER changes FX

One stream, two passes

Both passes draw from the same generator, ORDER first. Move ORDER density and the FX pass sees different numbers, so the effects re-deal too. That is by design: a pattern is one roll, not two independent ones.

Density is a chance, not a count

50% is about half

Each slice rolls on its own, so 50% FX on 32 slices gives around 16 effected slices, not exactly 16. Short loops with big slices have few cells and swing the most.

Re-roll vs seed

Tap walks, turn jumps

Tapping CAPTURE advances a counter that is mixed into the seed: same seed, next pattern. Turning SEED resets that counter, so the pot always lands on a seed's own first pattern. The pot reaches 128 of the engine's four billion seeds.

LENGTH and SLICE set how many cells there are: steps in the loop times two, divided by half-steps per slice, from 2 up to 512. Changing either re-rolls everything, because the stream is walked once per slice. In Dual mode the second channel's lane is rolled straight after the first from the same stream, so one seed still fixes both.

The 26 effects, and the punch

Every one of these is a roll candidate and a PUNCH FX choice on SETUP P4

A rolled effect gets a setting from the roll, listed in the third column. Held from B2, the effect is applied to every slice in the original order with its canonical setting, in the last column. CLEAN, at the bottom of the SETUP list, punches the pattern out: original order, no effects, for as long as you hold.

#EffectWhat it does to a sliceRolled settingAs a punch

Pitch and P-Shift roll their semitones within PITCH RANGE on the SETUP page, up or down; every other setting has a fixed menu. The four newest effects, P-Shift, Ring Mod, Comb and Scatter, joined the roll pool in the engine's v0.11.0, which reshuffled what older seeds produce; nothing on this module predates that.

The clock

06 — J3, two jobs, one setting

This module wants two things from a jack: a tempo, and a "capture now" trigger. J3 is that jack, and by default it works out which is arriving.

Default · Settings CLOCK IN = Auto

Auto

Worked out from what arrives: a steady train reads as a clock, sporadic hits read as capture triggers with the tempo inferred from them. Nothing to set.

Settings CLOCK IN = Clock

Always a clock

For a deliberately uneven clock, swung or gated, that you still want read as a clock rather than as triggers.

SETUP P3

Ratio

÷2, =1 or ×2 on whatever arrives, so a 16th-note clock and a quarter-note clock both work.

With nothing patched it free-runs at the TEMPO pot on the SETUP page. When a clock locks, the locked tempo becomes the new free-run default and the TEMPO pot adopts it, with catch, so unpatching later carries on at the tempo you were just at, and it is remembered across power cycles.

Internally, gate edges become 24 ppqn MIDI clock, because that is what the engine already speaks, so the timing model that runs on the Move runs here untouched. It is the piece with the most design risk in the port and the most thoroughly tested: a synthetic gate train drives the real engine to capture a bar of 95,985 frames against a 96,000 target, an error of 0.02%.

J3 is read raw in the audio callback, not through the SDK's 20 Hz pot filter, which would smear a pulse. It fires on the magnitude of the swing away from the jack's calibrated zero, 1.5 V to assert and 0.5 V to release, so a pulse counts whichever way the front end signs it. This is the one part of the port that native tests cannot reach, and a clock on J3 has not yet been tried on the racked module. Use a pulse of 5 ms or longer: the jack is read once per 128-sample block, so a trigger under about 3 ms can fall between two reads and be missed.

Every knob under CV

07 — J4 to J8, independent of the pots

On the Versio a CV input is summed with its knob in analogue, so the module can never read the jack alone. The Alchemy Lab reads pots and jacks separately and the SDK adds them in software, per page and per pot, which means a jack can drive a value on the SETUP page while the PLAY page is showing.

SEED under CV is the one to patch first: a slow LFO or a sequencer into J8 walks the pattern through the seed space, one arrangement per step, without touching the loop. FX and ORDER under CV is the thing this hardware does that the Move version cannot. None of J4 to J8 has been tried on the racked module yet.

The routing is fixed in v0.1.0. Two things the platform makes possible and this version does not yet do: choosing the routing from the Settings pages, and a CV output, a per-slice trigger out of one of these same jacks, which no other platform this engine runs on can offer.

The boot report

The one number this project needs

You cannot watch a status light while playing with both hands, so the module measures its own worst-case load while you play and shows it on the P1 ring for the first two and a half seconds of the next power-up, when you can actually look.

3%
measured on the bench, idle pass-through

A single dim blue LED means it has not measured anything yet. Green is under 75%, amber under 90%, red means it did not fit. Play hard, FX and ORDER up, half-step SLICE, long LENGTH, then power down and up. On the Versio the same engine read 50 to 75% on the same silicon; the SDK adds a 1 ms control poll and the LED driver on top, and the 3.3% read so far is idle pass-through, not a loop with effects. This number is still open.

Firmware on the card

09 — Carry several firmwares, change between them from the panel

Hermetic's firmware treats the SD card as a place for presets and files. This one also reads .bin files from it, and can write one into the module's firmware slot, verify it, and reboot into it, from a settings page, with the module on and in the rack. Mark and Belt for the Alchemy Lab carry the same page, and each of the three keeps its working state in a preset slot of its own, so switching away and back finds Smack as you left it.

Prepare the card

FAT32, a folder named alchemy

32 GB or under, MBR partition table, FAT32. Make a folder called alchemy in the root and put firmware images in it: any .bin built for the Alchemy Lab bootloader, this one included. On a Mac, run dot_clean on the card before ejecting so the picker does not list resource forks.

Or over USB

Without pulling the card

Files reach the card over USB while the module runs: the web programmer's file panel, or the repository's tools/hostlink-fs.mjs, which makes the folder and puts the images in it from a terminal. Uploads are staged and renamed onto their final name only after the firmware has checked the CRC.

The gesture

Hold B2 + B3 two seconds · tap B1 to the Firmware page

What it refuses, and what it never touches

It checks the image's first eight bytes before erasing anything and refuses one that is not bootable from SRAM: a build for internal flash would leave the bootloader with nothing to boot. The largest file it will take is the app slot up to the calibration sector, so the board's calibration record and the preset store are never in the erase path.

If a write fails, the module blinks red and boots nothing; the bootloader is intact, and hold-B3-at-power-on still gets you a USB flash. What this cannot protect you from is a file that is valid, bootable and wrong for the hardware. Keep the Patch card's modules folder and this card's alchemy folder apart.

The erase runs one 64 KB sector per frame and the write one 4 KB chunk per frame, between LED frames; the verify compares the slot through the memory-mapped view, then hands over to the bootloader with the grace window skipped so the new firmware simply starts. Nothing this firmware runs lives in the slot it is rewriting, so audio is meant to keep passing, though a sector erase blocks for a good fraction of a second and the rings will stutter through it.

Before any of that, the picker reads the file against the slot through the same memory-mapped view. If every byte matches, it stops there: picking the firmware that is already running costs nothing, and a loop in progress keeps playing. The first byte that differs hands over to the erase, write and verify, from the top of the file.

What has run. On 22 September 2026, on a racked module, the picker listed the card's folder and switched between Smack, Mark and Belt, and Smack came back with its own settings; picking the one already loaded gave the solid green ring and no reboot. The failure paths, a refused image and a failed write, have been reasoned through against the memory map but not exercised.

Build it

10 — No release yet; the source is the download

There is no packaged release of v0.1.0 yet: it has been played on one module, and the clock and CV inputs are still untried. Building takes a few minutes with the ARM toolchain installed.

git clone --recurse-submodules 
cd smack-alchemy
make libdaisy        # once
make                 # build/smack_alchemy.bin, ~357 KB
make test            # the native suites: clock, allocator, DJ filter, ring stall

Needs arm-none-eabi-gcc, make and dfu-util. libDaisy has nested submodules, so the clone must be recursive or the HAL is missing. make BENCH_USB=1 BUILD_DIR=build-bench builds a variant with HostLink on the Daisy Seed's own micro-USB, for a module out of its case on a bench bootloader; it is not the one to rack.

Installing

11 — Through the front USB-C, and getting back to stock

The Alchemy Lab's bootloader serves DFU on the front-panel USB-C, so the module stays in the rack. Once this firmware is on it, so does every update after.

First time

Hold B3 at power-on

During the two-second boot window the rings run a warm-white comet. Hold B3 through it and they switch to a slow breathe with the B3 lights on: update mode. Then flash the .bin with Hermetic's web programmer, or from the repository:

make program-dfu
Every time after

Live

With Smack running, the module can be asked to reboot into update mode over HostLink and flashed in the same breath, no button held:

make program-live

Or put the new .bin on the card and use the picker.

Back to stock

Same door

Flashing any Alchemy Lab firmware, Hermetic's own included, the same way returns the module to it. Presets in the slots survive; this firmware's working state is in its own slot, the thirteenth of sixteen, and is schema-guarded, so another firmware will not read it as its own.

Before you flash it

It is custom firmware. Hermetic Modular did not write it, did not test it and cannot support it, so please do not contact them about it. It has been played on one module, its author's, and the clock and CV inputs have not yet been tried there. If you run it, the two things worth reporting are whether J3 locks to your clock and what the boot report reads after a hard session.

How it is built

12 — The parts worth knowing about

The engine was not touched. Everything below is the layer between it and the Alchemy SDK, and the handful of decisions that shaped that layer.

PieceWhat it is
Two pages, one layerThe SDK's Pager makes SETUP a held layer on B3 with pot catch on the way in and out. Nothing on the PLAY page can jump because you looked at the other one.
Working presetSmack's own slot, the thirteenth of sixteen (12 counting from zero; Mark, Belt and Relay have the three after it), holds the live state: both pages, the Settings and two extras, the free-run tempo and the CPU peak. It autosaves five seconds after the last change on either page with hands off the buttons and the picker idle, one 4 KB sector per save, behind epsilons so a drifting number cannot wear the flash. At boot the home slot wins; a module whose state was saved by an older build in the first slot carries it over once. Loops are never saved.
The pickerThree Custom slots on a Settings page: a file scanner, a state machine that compares, then erases, writes and verifies, one step per frame, and a DFU hand-off. Shared, by copy, with Mark and Belt. Its buffers live in AXI SRAM because the SD controller's DMA cannot reach the tightly-coupled memory the rest of the firmware's data uses.
Clock adapterGate edges become 24 ppqn MIDI clock, three tiers (external, inferred, free-run) with auto-detection. Seven native tests, unchanged from the Versio.
Ring recorder150 seconds, sized to hold two of the longest loop, so the loop that is playing is protected from the recorder.
SDRAM allocatorA bump pool in external RAM, 27.6 MB of 32 MB, so the engine's four callocs land there with no engine edits.
Engine at 48 kHz128-frame blocks, as the engine's clock regression requires; the SDK's default block is 24, so the firmware asks for 128 at init.
DJ filterTwo cascaded one-poles per channel, and a true bypass at the notch: at exactly zero the block is skipped and the filter state cleared, so re-engaging starts from silence.
HostLinkProduct "Smack", both pages, ten jacks, three buttons, a manual and the SD filesystem, over the front USB-C. Verified on the bench: the module identifies itself, serves its descriptor, and its slot table and live state read back correctly.
Budget~357 KB image. SRAM 74%, DTCM 61%, SDRAM 50%. CPU: 3.3% peak measured at idle pass-through; a loop with effects is not yet measured.

Found something, or want it to do something it doesn't? Open an issue. A report from someone playing it is worth more than anything measurable on a laptop.
